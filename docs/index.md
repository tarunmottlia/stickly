# Choosing referral program software by payout model: cash, per-referral or product credits

*By Tarun Kumar · 15 November 2026*

Feature grids will not pick your referral program software — the payout model will. Every tool pays in one of three ways: a cash share of the revenue a referral brings, a fixed reward per referral, or credits in your own product. The model sets the tooling, the cost shape and the fraud risk, so pick it first and the shortlist writes itself. Prices below are as listed on each tool's site on 14 Nov 2026.

## The three payout models

- **Cash share of revenue.** Referrers get a cut of the recurring bill from each customer they send. Fits high-ACV B2B, partners and affiliates.
- **Fixed reward per referral.** One referral earns one reward — gift card, cash or credits, paid once. Fits consumer and prosumer apps with many referrers.
- **Product credits.** Users earn your product's own currency for actions inside it, invites included. Fits usage-priced products where a credit has a clear value.

## Cash share: Cello, Rewardful, Tolt

Cello is built for this: it attributes a signup to a referrer, works out a share of that customer's recurring revenue and pays the referrer real money through PayPal or Venmo. Plans: Start at $65/mo (up to $10K referral ARR), Grow at $200/mo (up to $50K), Scale at $750/mo (up to $100K), Enterprise custom.

Affiliate tools share this model — the affiliate vs referral distinction is about who promotes you, outside partners versus your own users, not how anyone gets paid. Rewardful runs affiliate and referral programs through its Stripe integration and is a Premier Stripe Partner, from $49/mo for up to $7,500/mo in affiliate-attributed revenue, with a 14-day free trial and a 0% transaction fee on affiliate-attributed revenue. Tolt: Growth at $99/mo for up to $20k/mo in partner revenue, Pro at $199/mo for up to $50k, automatic payouts via PayPal, Wise, bank and wire at 2% of what is paid out, plus a 14-day trial with no card.

## Per referral: GrowSurf

GrowSurf rewards users with gift cards, credits, cash and more, and also supports affiliate programs; it ships embeddable elements plus JavaScript, iOS, Android and React Native SDKs. Startup is $125/mo on yearly billing for up to 2,500 participants, Business is $314/mo, there is a 14-day free trial, and fraud protection is listed. That fraud line matters here: when a large crowd can each earn a reward, some will refer themselves with a second email. Low-priced SaaS usually lands in this model — a revenue share on a small monthly bill rarely justifies the payout overhead.

## Product credits: RewardBase and Stickly

RewardBase runs reward programs for user actions — referrals, reviews, feedback — natively inside your product, with users submitting URLs, images or text that you review and approve before a reward is issued. It has a free plan at $0/mo for 100 participants a month; example rewards: complete onboarding for 100 AI credits, invite 3 team members for 20% off renewal, submit a feature request for 100 AI credits.

Stickly removes the review step: every task is a link — the user clicks, the tab opens, credits land, an honour system. Tasks include invite a friend, plus custom links you write yourself. Payout goes through a signed webhook; Stickly never holds money and does no fraud handling beyond server-signed identity, and live keys require a JWT your server signs, so the user id never comes from the browser. It is free up to 1,000 participants a month with the Stickly badge and no card, Pro is $19/mo for 10,000 monthly participants, and Lifetime is $199 one-time for 10,000. The trade-off is verification; the payoff is that product credits cost margin, not money.

## Five questions before you book a demo

1. **Cash or margin?**
2. **Inside your product or outside it?**
3. **Attribution to revenue over months, or a one-time count?**
4. **Who handles fraud?** GrowSurf lists fraud protection; RewardBase has you approve submissions; Stickly relies on a server-signed JWT, nothing more.
5. **Platform rules?** GitHub's Acceptable Use Policy bans activity incentivized by credits or gifts, and Trustpilot bans incentivized reviews — so never reward stars, reviews on those sites, upvotes or social follows.
